How an Output Is Created

This is how expertise turns into actionable proposals.

The pipeline from assignment to published output — without which expertise is just an email in an inbox.

1

Assignment

An expert defines the problem, the hypothesis, and the expected output.

2

Research & data

A junior analyst works with primary sources, data, legislation, and international comparisons.

3

Mentoring & QA

A senior expert reviews methodology, facts, logic, and recommendations. (QA = quality assurance — expert review before publication.)

4

Publication & communication

The output becomes a policy brief, explainer, graphic, roundtable input, or material for public debate.

Transparency and Independence

Your support builds infrastructure, not political influence.

FSF is a non-partisan, independent, evidence-based think tank. In private partnerships we strictly separate support for the organisation from influence over content. A donor does not have the right to determine expert conclusions.

Every partnership goes through conflict-of-interest screening and a reputational review. For larger private partnerships we respect internal source-diversification rules (private sponsorship is capped at 25 % of the total budget).

Partnership does NOT mean influence over expert conclusions or buying content.
Partnership does NOT mean political favors.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

Is FSF a political organization?

No. FSF is a non-partisan, independent, evidence-based think tank. Partnership with us does not mean support for any political party.

Can a corporate partner influence the content of a policy brief?

No. A corporate partner or foundation can support capacity building or a specific topic that aligns with the FSF mission, but can never intervene in expert conclusions, data, or methodology.

Can I support just one specific area?

Yes. A partner can support a specific area or type of capacity — for example analytical talent, a policy brief, or the bridge fund. They cannot, however, determine expert conclusions, methodology, or the content of the output itself.

Will our company receive a contract and a receipt?

Of course. For corporate partnerships we conclude a standard donation agreement (or a collaboration agreement depending on the form of support) that meets all accounting and legal requirements.

Do you publish the names of donors?

Depending on the agreement, type of support, and legal obligations. For corporate and foundation partnerships we prefer transparent communication, while respecting reasonable confidentiality rules where justified.
